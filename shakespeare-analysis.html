Shakespeare Text Analysis

Python programs that quantify word frequency and thematic language across Shakespeare's plays

Project Overview

A pair of Python programs that turn Shakespeare's plays into data. The first measures the most frequent words in any single play; the second scores curated thematic vocabularies — Love, Power, Revenge, and Hate — across entire genres to compare how comedies, tragedies, and histories use language differently. Both run over a corpus of 20+ full play texts and produce CSV data and charts.

Two Programs

  • Word-frequency analyzer: the user picks a play; the program tokenizes and cleans the text, builds a word → count dictionary, ranks the top 15 words, writes them to a CSV, and renders a pie chart of the results as a PDF.
  • Thematic analyzer: starts from hand-built keyword sets for four themes (Love, Power, Revenge, Hate), then counts how often each theme's vocabulary appears across a chosen genre (Comedies, Tragedies, or Histories). It reports the theme distribution and the top thematic words as a CSV plus a two-panel figure — a bar chart of theme totals and a pie chart of the leading words.

How It Works

  • Reads raw play texts, then lowercases and strips punctuation with string and re so words are counted consistently.
  • Uses Python dictionaries to accumulate counts, and sorted() with a lambda key to rank results.
  • Organizes the corpus by genre so thematic comparisons are made across comparable groups of plays.
  • Exports results with the csv module and visualizes them with matplotlib, saving publication-ready PDF charts.

Tools & Technologies

  • Python — text processing and analysis
  • string & re — tokenization and cleaning
  • csv — structured data export
  • matplotlib — bar and pie chart visualization

What I Learned

  • How dictionaries and sorting make short work of counting and ranking over large bodies of text.
  • That the interesting design choice in thematic analysis is the keyword set — building good vocabularies for abstract themes is harder than the counting itself.
  • How to move from raw text to clean CSV data to a readable chart in one pipeline.

Project Overview

SquashLab is an iOS app that analyzes a squash player's swing from a single phone video and returns coaching feedback — no sensors, no wearables. A player films a rally, the app detects each swing, measures body and racket mechanics through the swing, and scores it. Under the hood it combines pose estimation with a self-trained racket-detection model and a multi-signal shot splitter, running heavier inference in the cloud while capturing high-frame-rate video on-device.

How It Works

  • Capture: the iOS app records high-frame-rate video on-device and normalizes every upload to a consistent frame rate so downstream analysis stays stable.
  • Shot splitting: a multi-signal splitter fuses wrist speed, racket position, and audio (the sound of ball contact) to segment a rally into individual swings — replacing a fragile fixed-window approach.
  • Pose & racket tracking: a body-pose model plus a custom-trained racket detector locate the player and racket frame by frame.
  • Metrics: phase-based mechanics (body rotation, knee bend, contact point) are measured at the meaningful moments of each swing.
  • Scoring & feedback: a Swing Score and Session Score summarize performance, presented in a coach's tone rather than as raw numbers.

Engineering Highlights

  • Trained a custom RF-DETR racket-detection model and validated it against a hand-labeled ground-truth set, where it outperformed the previous YOLO-based detector.
  • Built a multi-signal shot splitter (motion + racket + audio contact) to reliably isolate swings, the hardest part of the pipeline.
  • Diagnosed weak racket detection as a capture problem (motion blur and low frame rate) rather than a model problem, and fixed it with higher-fps capture and blur augmentation.
  • Designed a data flywheel: uploads feed a labeling loop that continuously improves the models.
  • Shipped an end-to-end production pipeline — on-device capture, cloud GPU inference, and results delivered back to the phone with live progress updates.

Tools & Technologies

  • Swift / SwiftUI — native iOS app (on-device capture, results UI, live progress)
  • Python — computer-vision pipeline (pose estimation, shot splitting, metrics)
  • RF-DETR & YOLO — racket detection (custom-trained and validated)
  • MediaPipe — body-pose estimation
  • Modal — GPU-backed cloud inference
  • Fly.io — pipeline / API server
  • Supabase — backend, authentication (Sign in with Apple), and data

What I Learned

  • That data quality and capture conditions often matter more than model architecture — the biggest wins came from better video, not bigger models.
  • How to validate a model against real ground truth instead of trusting benchmark metrics, and how to retire features that ground truth showed weren't working.
  • How to design a full ML product: mobile capture, cloud inference, storage, auth, and a feedback loop that keeps improving the models.
  • How to collaborate on a shared codebase with disciplined version control.
  • How to grow a product: I reached out to more than 100 professional players for feedback and promotion.
